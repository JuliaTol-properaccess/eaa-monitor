The Monday measurement

Seven sectors, one question: is the statement there?

Every Monday morning we automatically check whether organisations have an accessibility statement in their footer: web shops, financial institutions, telecom providers, passenger carriers, media services, e-book platforms and travel companies. Each sector has its own dashboard and its own regulator. The figures are never more than a week old.

Important: an accessibility statement is a promise, not proof. Whether a website really works for people with a disability only shows in a substantive test of the site itself. This monitor only checks whether the statement is in the footer, not whether it is accurate or how accessible the site really is.

Frequently asked questions

What is the European Accessibility Act? +

The European Accessibility Act requires commercial organisations that offer products or services to consumers to make their digital channels accessible. In the Netherlands the law has been in force since 28 June 2025. One of the requirements is publishing an accessibility statement on the website, describing how accessible the service is and what steps the organisation is taking.

Does the EAA apply to my web shop too? +

Not every site is covered. The law targets businesses that sell products or services to consumers online. Micro-enterprises offering services, with fewer than ten employees and less than two million euros in turnover, are exempt. In doubt? A few questions usually make it clear.

What are the fines if I do nothing? +

The ACM supervises web shops and can impose fines up to € 900,000 or 1% of annual turnover. For the financial sector the AFM is the regulator. A notification duty has also applied since October 2025. No enforcement cases are known yet in the Netherlands, but the first procedures are already under way in France and Germany, among others.

Is an accessibility statement enough? +

No. A statement describes how accessible your site is, but does not make it accessible. The law requires that your site actually works for people with a disability. The statement is the finishing touch of the work, not the solution. An honest statement also names what does not work yet and when you will fix it.

Do accessibility overlays work? +

A single line of code that adds a button or widget does not solve the underlying problems. Overlays often even introduce new barriers and are usually switched off by experienced assistive-technology users. Real accessibility comes from fixing problems in the source code and content, not from adding a layer on top.

How does the EAA Monitor measure this? +

Every Monday the monitor automatically checks the footer of Dutch web shops, financial institutions, telecom providers, carriers, media services, e-book platforms and travel companies for a link to an accessibility statement. We look for keywords such as toegankelijkheid and accessibility in link text and URL. Note: a link does not mean the site is actually accessible. It is a first indication of awareness and compliance.
